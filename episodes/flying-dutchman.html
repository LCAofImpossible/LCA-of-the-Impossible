MYTHS & LEGENDS · EPISODE #35

The Flying
Dutchman

What would it take in materials, energy and greenhouse-gas emissions to build a plausible seventeenth-century Flying Dutchman and condemn it to one year of endless navigation?

528.2 t CO₂eone year of cursed navigation, including full construction, maintenance and auxiliary operation

THE RECONSTRUCTION

A ship that
refused to arrive.

The legend has no authenticated vessel or blueprint, so the physical ship is reconstructed as a large early-seventeenth-century Dutch ocean-going merchant vessel of the VOC tradition. Batavia provides the primary analogue for dimensions, timber, rigging, sails and armament.

The curse belongs to the narrative. The body of the ship belongs to the inventory.

01 · FUNCTIONAL UNIT

One cursed year

Construction of one plausible Flying Dutchman and one year of cursed navigation, with the entire construction burden assigned to that first year.

02 · HOTSPOT

Construction: 97.4%

Almost the entire first-year footprint arrives before the ship begins its endless voyage. Metal armament, anchors, fasteners and fittings are the largest hotspot.

03 · SENSITIVITY

34.1 t CO₂e/year

Spreading construction across 25 years reduces the annualised result dramatically while leaving the physical inventory unchanged.

THE VERDICT

The ship may never reach harbour.
Its footprint arrived before it ever left.
The sea forgets every wake.
Carbon does not.

Narrative engineering reconstruction for communication purposes. DEFRA 2026 factors cover approximately 95.6% of quantified GWP; this is not a verified ISO LCA.
