EPISODE #35 · MYTH & LEGENDS

The Flying Dutchman

What is the carbon footprint of a ship condemned to sail forever?

528.2 t CO₂e one year of continuous cursed navigation, including full vessel construction, one year of maintenance and auxiliary use

THE SUBJECT

A ship that never reaches harbour.

The Flying Dutchman is reconstructed as a large early-17th-century Dutch East Indiaman. Batavia provides the main physical analogy, while Vasa acts as a contemporary scale check. The curse belongs to the story; the body of the ship is reconstructed from historical evidence.

01

Geometry

Approx. 45 m between posts, 56 m overall with bowsprit, 10.5 m beam and a main mast around 55 m high.

02

Navigation

Three-masted full rig. At the modelling speed of 5 knots, one cursed year corresponds to roughly 81,000 km.

03

Main hotspot

Metal armament, anchors, fasteners and fittings are the largest individual contributor.

04

Modelling convention

The full construction burden is charged to the first year. A 25-year sensitivity is shown separately.

Included

Hull, decks, masts, sheathing, armament, fittings, sails, rigging, ballast, coatings, supply transport, shipyard heat, annual repairs and auxiliary fires.

Excluded

The curse, crew resurrection, metabolism, provisions, storm generation, spectral light, cargo and end of life. A ship that cannot die has no final disposal.

Emission-factor logic

95.6% of quantified GWP uses DEFRA 2026 factors. Flax/hemp uses a literature proxy because DEFRA has no specific sailcloth or natural-cordage factor.

25-year sensitivity

Spreading the construction burden across 25 years reduces the annualised result to about 34.1 t CO₂e/year.

Key interpretation

The Dutchman is not defined by fuel. It is defined by mass: metal alone carries more than half of the total footprint.

THE VERDICT

The ship may never reach harbour.
Its footprint arrived before it ever left.
The sea forgets every wake.
Carbon does not.
