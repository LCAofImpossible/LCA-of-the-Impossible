MYTHS & LEGENDS · EPISODE #36

The Tower
of Babel

What is the carbon footprint of a monument designed to reach heaven?

22,491 t CO₂eone completed 90 m tower for one year, with full construction assigned to that year

THE RECONSTRUCTION

A staircase
to heaven.

The biblical story gives the material language: bricks hardened by fire and bitumen instead of mortar. Archaeology gives the scale. The case uses Neo-Babylonian Etemenanki as the physical analogue for a completed seven-stage ziggurat approximately 91.5 m square at the base and about 90 m high.

The surrounding city, demolition and divine intervention sit outside the base system boundary. The latter is narratively decisive but produces no quantifiable physical flow, so it is not modelled.

01 · FUNCTIONAL UNIT

One completed tower

Provision and operation of one completed 90 m Tower of Babel for one year, with the full construction burden assigned to the year.

02 · HOTSPOT

Fired-brick skin

The fired-brick envelope contributes approximately 95.4% of the total result. The enormous mudbrick core is not the climate hotspot.

03 · SENSITIVITY

262 t CO₂e/year

When construction is spread across a 100-year life, the annualised result falls to about 262 t CO₂e per year.

THE VERDICT

They tried to give one language to the sky.
The kilns answered first.
The words were scattered.
The carbon stayed.

Narrative engineering reconstruction for communication purposes. Contemporary emission factors are used as modern-equivalent proxies; this is not a verified ISO LCA.
