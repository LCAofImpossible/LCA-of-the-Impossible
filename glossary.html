LCA GLOSSARY

Technical language,
without the fog.

The series uses real Life Cycle Assessment terminology. This glossary keeps that language precise while making the concepts accessible to readers who do not work with LCA every day.

SEARCH TERMS

Filter by term or definition.
AActivity data
The quantified physical or operational input used in a calculation, such as kilograms of material, kilometres travelled, kilowatt-hours consumed or number of repeated events.
AAllocation
The partitioning of inputs, outputs or environmental burdens between multiple products or functions when a process serves more than one output and the burden cannot otherwise be separated.
AAssumption
A declared modelling choice used where the available evidence cannot fully determine a parameter, scenario, duration, distance, frequency or technical configuration.
BBiogenic CO₂
Carbon dioxide associated with biological carbon cycles. Depending on the factor set and reporting framework, it may be reported separately from fossil greenhouse-gas emissions.
CCarbon footprint
The climate-change impact associated with the defined product system and reporting basis, normally expressed as mass of CO₂ equivalent.
CClimate change
The impact category used in the series to express greenhouse-gas emissions as carbon-dioxide equivalents using an appropriate characterization basis.
CCut-off
A rule or modelling decision defining which flows, processes or contributions may be excluded from the system because of relevance, data limitations or an established study criterion.
DDirect emissions
Emissions released directly by the activity being represented, such as fuel combustion in a vehicle or process, as distinct from upstream fuel-production burdens.
EEmission factor
A coefficient that converts activity data into greenhouse-gas emissions, for example kg CO₂e per litre of fuel, tonne-kilometre, kWh or kilogram of material.
EEvidence confidence
A qualitative editorial indicator describing how strongly narrative, historical or physical evidence constrains the reconstruction. In this project it is expressed only as Low, Medium or High and is not a formal ISO data-quality score.
FFunctional unit
The quantified function or service to which the life-cycle result refers. It makes clear what is being delivered, for how much, for how long or under which service condition.
HHotspot
A life-cycle stage, material, process or activity that contributes disproportionately to the total result and therefore controls the environmental interpretation.
IInventory / LCI
The quantified set of material, energy, transport, operation, maintenance and other flows used to represent the modelled system before impact calculation.
LLife Cycle Assessment / LCA
A structured method for assessing environmental inputs, outputs and impacts across a defined product system in relation to a stated function and system boundary.
MModelling basis
The reason a quantity, process or factor has been represented in a particular way: direct evidence, engineering reconstruction, representative data, analogue or explicit assumption.
OOutside of scopes
A separately reported emissions component used by some greenhouse-gas factor sets, commonly associated with biogenic carbon treatment. It should not be silently added to or removed from the main result.
PProxy
A substitute dataset, process or analogue used when a sufficiently specific representation of the modelled flow is unavailable.
PProxy dependence
A qualitative indicator of how strongly an episode depends on representative or analogue datasets standing in for unknown or impossible processes. It is expressed as Low, Medium or High.
RReporting basis
The quantified slice of an analysis used to communicate the result when a conventional functional unit is not practical — for example one journey, one event, one operating cycle or a defined lifetime.
SSensitivity analysis
A deliberate variation of an uncertain parameter or modelling choice to test how strongly the result or interpretation changes.
SSystem boundary
The definition of which life-cycle stages, processes and flows are included in the model and which remain outside it.
TTransmission & distribution / T&D
Losses and associated impacts that occur when electricity is transmitted and distributed between generation and the point of use, when these components are relevant to the factor set and boundary.
UUncertainty
The limitation in knowledge about a parameter, source, scenario or model. In the series, uncertainty is made visible through assumptions, evidence profiles and sensitivities rather than hidden behind extra decimal places.
WWell-to-tank / WTT
The upstream greenhouse-gas burden associated with extracting, processing and delivering a fuel or energy carrier before its direct use.
